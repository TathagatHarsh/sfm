⚠️ Frequent Exam Traps

• Beta of Market: Is always 1.0. Beta of Risk-free asset is always 0.

• IRR Trap: If cash flows change signs more than once, there are multiple IRRs. IRR is unreliable for mutually exclusive projects of different sizes.

• TV g-rate: Gordon Growth Model breaks if g ≥ WACC. In reality, g cannot exceed GDP growth rate in perpetuity.

The CAPM Formula

The Capital Asset Pricing Model defines the relationship between systematic risk and expected return for assets.

E(Ri) = Rf + βi [E(Rm) − Rf]
Wait, what is [Rm - Rf]?
This is called the Market Risk Premium. It is the extra return investors demand for shifting their money from a safe bond (Rf) to the risky stock market (Rm).

Two Types of Risk

Modern Portfolio Theory splits risk into two components:

1. Systematic (Market) Risk
Risk that affects ALL stocks (recession, interest rates, war).
Diversification? Cannot be eliminated.
Metric: Beta (β)
2. Unsystematic (Firm) Risk
Risk specific to one company (strike, bad CEO, lawsuit).
Diversification? Can be eliminated for free.
Reward? Market pays ZERO premium for this risk.

Terminal Value (TV)

Since we cannot forecast cash flows to infinity, we assume the company reaches a "steady state" after year 5 or 10 and grows at a constant rate (g) forever.

TVn = [FCFFn+1] / (WACC − g)

Crucial Point: In the exam, if you are given FCFF for year 5, you must multiply it by (1+g) to get FCFF for year 6 before using the formula.

⚠️ The Financing Project Trap

If you get money UPFRONT (inflow at t=0) and pay later (outflows), the IRR rule reverses.

Financing Project: Accept only if IRR < WACC.
Think of it as a loan: You want the "interest rate" (IRR) of your borrowing to be lower than your cost of capital.

Multiple IRRs

Every time the sign of cash flows changes, there is potentially a new IRR (Descartes' Rule of Signs).

If signs go: (−, +, −) → 2 IRRs. In such cases, ignore IRR and only use NPV.

Computing FCFF

FCFF is the cash available to ALL capital providers (Debt + Equity).

FCFF = [EBIT × (1−t)] + Dep − Capex − ΔNWC
Intuition:
1. EBIT(1-t): Core operating profit after tax.
2. + Dep: Add back non-cash expenses.
3. - Capex: Subtract cash spent on fixed assets.
4. - ΔNWC: Subtract cash "locked" in working capital.

The NWC Logic

Net Working Capital = Current Assets − Current Liabilities.

In cash flow (FCFF): Increase in NWC is SUBTRACTED. Because if inventory (an asset) goes up, you spent cash to buy it.
In reinvestment rate: ΔNWC is added because it represents cash going INTO the business to support higher future sales. It reduces current FCFF but enables future growth.

Quick example from Q15

NWC = 1% of revenue each year.
Year 1: Revenue=10,000 → NWC=100 → ΔNWC = 100 (subtract)
Year 2: Revenue=11,000 → NWC=110 → ΔNWC = 10 (subtract)
Year 4: Project ends → all NWC recovered → +total NWC as inflow

📋 Complete Formula Reference

CAPM & Risk
Expected Return = Rf + β × (Rm − Rf)
Beta = Cov(Ri, Rm) / Var(Rm) = [Excess return of stock / Excess return of market]
Market Risk Premium = Rm − Rf (market-wide constant)
DCF & Terminal Value
Firm Value = Σ FCFFt/(1+WACC)t + TV/(1+WACC)n
Terminal Value = FCFFn × (1+g) / (WACC − g)
FCFF
FCFF (EBIT method) = EBIT×(1−t) + Dep − ΔNWC − Capex
FCFF (NI method) = Net Income + Dep + Interest×(1−t) − ΔNWC − Capex
DuPont
ROE = Net Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier
EM = ROE / (NPM × ATO) = Total Assets / Shareholders' Equity
Reinvestment
Reinvestment Rate = (Capex − Dep + ΔNWC) / NOPAT
Sustainable Growth = ROC × Reinvestment Rate
